# The Founding of Rome: From Myth to Republic

## Overview

The origins of Rome are shrouded in legend, blending mythological narrative with historical reality. Traditional accounts placed Rome's founding in 753 BCE by Romulus and Remus, descendants of the Trojan hero Aeneas. Modern archaeology reveals a more complex picture of gradual urban development from Latin and Sabine villages. This chapter explores both the legends that shaped Roman identity and the historical processes that created the Roman city-state.

## The Legendary Origins

### Aeneas and the Trojan Heritage

Roman tradition traced the city's origins to Troy through Aeneas:
- **Aeneas**: Trojan prince, son of Anchises and Venus
- Fled burning Troy with father, son Ascanius, and household gods
- Wandered the Mediterranean before reaching Italy
- Founded Lavinium in Latium
- His son Ascanius (Iulus) founded Alba Longa

Virgil's *Aeneid* would later immortalize this legend, linking Rome to the epic tradition of Homer.

### Romulus and Remus

The canonical foundation legend:

**The Twins' Birth**:
- Numitor, king of Alba Longa, was deposed by his brother Amulius
- Amulius forced Numitor's daughter Rhea Silvia to become a Vestal Virgin
- Mars seduced Rhea Silvia; she bore twins Romulus and Remus
- Amulius ordered the twins exposed by the Tiber

**Rescue and Upbringing**:
- The twins were suckled by a she-wolf (*lupa*) in the Lupercal cave
- Found by the shepherd Faustulus and raised in his household
- Grew up as shepherds, showing natural leadership

**The Foundation (753 BCE)**:
- As adults, the twins killed Amulius and restored Numitor
- Decided to found a new city on the Tiber
- Disputed which hill should be the center (Romulus favored Palatine, Remus the Aventine)
- Augury favored Romulus (12 vultures vs. 6)
- Romulus plowed the sacred boundary (*pomerium*)
- Remus mocked the walls by jumping over them
- Romulus killed Remus: "So perish whoever else shall leap over my walls"

### The Early Kings

Roman tradition listed seven kings (753-509 BCE):

1. **Romulus** (753-716 BCE): Founded Rome; created Senate; Rape of the Sabine Women
2. **Numa Pompilius** (715-673 BCE): Sabine king; established religious institutions
3. **Tullus Hostilius** (673-642 BCE): Warlike; destroyed Alba Longa
4. **Ancus Marcius** (642-617 BCE): Built port at Ostia
5. **Tarquinius Priscus** (616-579 BCE): Etruscan; began temple of Jupiter
6. **Servius Tullius** (578-535 BCE): Census; Servian constitution
7. **Tarquinius Superbus** (535-509 BCE): Tyrannical; expelled

The last three kings show strong Etruscan influence on early Rome.

## Archaeological Evidence

### The Site of Rome

Rome's location was strategic:
- On the Tiber River, the boundary between Latium and Etruria
- At the lowest practical crossing point
- Seven hills (Palatine, Aventine, Capitoline, Quirinal, Viminal, Esquiline, Caelian) provided defense
- Salt trade route (Via Salaria) passed nearby

### Early Settlement

Archaeological discoveries:
- **10th century BCE**: Hut foundations on the Palatine
- **8th century BCE**: Earliest burials; traces of organized settlement
- **7th century BCE**: Draining of the Forum area; monumentalization
- **6th century BCE**: Temple of Jupiter Capitolinus begun; city walls

The legendary founding date (753 BCE) roughly corresponds to the archaeological evidence of nucleated settlement.

### Etruscan Influence

The Etruscans profoundly shaped early Rome:
- Urban planning and architecture
- Religious practices (divination, ritual)
- Regalia and symbols of power (fasces, curule chair)
- Alphabet and writing
- The very name "Roma" may be Etruscan

## Social Structure

### Patricians and Plebeians

Roman society was divided:

**Patricians** (*patricii*):
- Hereditary aristocracy
- Claimed descent from original senators
- Monopolized priesthoods and magistracies
- Controlled religious knowledge

**Plebeians** (*plebs*):
- The common people
- Excluded from highest offices and religious positions
- Included wealthy merchants and poor farmers
- Would struggle for equality in the early Republic

### The Family (*Familia*)

Roman society centered on the family:
- **Paterfamilias**: Male head with near-absolute power
- **Patria Potestas**: Father's power over children (even adult)
- **Gens**: Clan claiming common ancestor
- Ancestor worship and household gods (Lares, Penates)

## Religion

### Roman Religious Practices

Early Roman religion was:
- Animistic: *numina* (divine powers) in natural phenomena
- Contractual: correct ritual ensured divine favor
- Practical: focused on results, not theology

**Key Deities**:
- **Jupiter**: Sky god, king of gods
- **Mars**: God of war, agriculture; father of Romulus
- **Juno**: Queen of gods; protector of women
- **Vesta**: Goddess of the hearth; served by Vestal Virgins

### Priests and Ritual

- **Pontifex Maximus**: Chief priest
- **Augurs**: Interpreted bird omens
- **Flamines**: Priests of specific gods
- **Vestals**: Maintained sacred fire

## The Fall of the Monarchy (509 BCE)

### The Rape of Lucretia

Traditional account of the Republic's founding:
- Sextus Tarquinius, son of the king, raped the noblewoman Lucretia
- Lucretia revealed the crime and killed herself
- Her kinsman Lucius Junius Brutus led a revolt
- The Tarquins were expelled
- Romans swore never again to have kings

### Historical Reality

Modern scholars suggest:
- Growing aristocratic resistance to royal power
- Possible Etruscan power struggles
- Gradual rather than sudden transition
- The traditional date (509 BCE) may be approximate

### The New Republic

Two annually elected **consuls** replaced the king:
- Shared power prevented tyranny
- Colleagues could veto each other
- One-year terms prevented entrenchment
- Brutus and Collatinus were traditionally the first consuls
