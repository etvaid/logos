# The Fall of the Roman Republic: Civil War and Caesar

## Overview

The final century of the Roman Republic (133-27 BCE) witnessed violent political conflict, civil wars, and the emergence of powerful military commanders who would ultimately destroy the republican system. From the reforms of the Gracchi through the dictatorship of Julius Caesar to the rise of Augustus, this era saw the transformation of Rome from an aristocratic republic to a monarchical empire.

## The Crisis Begins

### Structural Problems

Rome's success created internal contradictions:
- **Land Concentration**: Wealthy aristocrats accumulated vast estates (*latifundia*)
- **Decline of Small Farmers**: Veterans returned to find their farms absorbed; drifted to Rome
- **Slave Labor**: War captives replaced free farmers
- **Army Loyalty**: Professional soldiers loyal to commanders, not state
- **Italian Discontent**: Allies demanded citizenship

### The Gracchi

**Tiberius Gracchus** (Tribune 133 BCE):
- Proposed redistribution of public land (*ager publicus*)
- Broke convention by deposing opposing tribune
- Killed by senators who claimed he sought kingship

**Gaius Gracchus** (Tribune 123-122 BCE):
- Broader reform program: grain subsidies, jury reform, citizen colonies
- Proposed Latin citizenship
- Also killed by senatorial violence

The Gracchi established a dangerous precedent: political violence to resolve disputes.

## Marius and Sulla

### Gaius Marius (157-86 BCE)

The "new man" from Arpinum revolutionized Roman warfare:
- Elected consul seven times (unprecedented)
- **Military Reforms**: Recruited landless citizens; standardized equipment; created professional army
- Soldiers now depended on commanders for land at discharge
- Victories in Africa (Jugurthine War) and against Germanic tribes (Cimbri and Teutones)

### The Social War (91-88 BCE)

Italian allies finally revolted for citizenship:
- Brutal conflict across Italy
- Rome eventually granted citizenship to all Italians
- Expanded citizen body transformed Roman politics

### Sulla (138-78 BCE)

Patrician general who marched on Rome:
- Disputed command against Mithridates of Pontus with Marius
- **First March on Rome (88 BCE)**: Unprecedented use of army against the city
- While Sulla was in the East, Marius seized Rome and massacred enemies
- **Second March and Proscriptions (82 BCE)**: Sulla as dictator; lists of enemies killed
- Constitutional reforms to strengthen Senate
- Retired voluntarily (79 BCE); died 78 BCE

Sulla demonstrated that military force could override republican institutions.

## The Rise of Pompey and Caesar

### Pompey the Great (106-48 BCE)

Rome's greatest general before Caesar:
- Crushed remnants of Marian faction; suppressed Spartacus revolt
- **Command against Pirates (67 BCE)**: Unprecedented powers; cleared Mediterranean
- **Command against Mithridates (66 BCE)**: Conquered the East; reorganized Asia Minor
- Returned to Italy seeking land for veterans

### The First Triumvirate (60 BCE)

Informal alliance of:
- **Pompey**: Military prestige; frustrated by Senate
- **Crassus**: Wealth; political ambition
- **Julius Caesar**: Political talent; ambition

Together they dominated Roman politics.

### Julius Caesar (100-44 BCE)

**The Gallic Wars (58-50 BCE)**:
- Consul 59 BCE; then governor of Gaul
- Conquered all of Gaul (modern France, Belgium)
- Invaded Britain twice; crossed the Rhine
- Created a veteran army personally loyal to him
- Wrote *Commentarii de Bello Gallico*—brilliant propaganda

**Crossing the Rubicon (49 BCE)**:
- Senate ordered Caesar to disband his army
- Caesar crossed the Rubicon River into Italy with his troops
- Civil war began

**Civil War (49-45 BCE)**:
- Caesar swiftly conquered Italy
- Pompey fled to Greece; defeated at **Pharsalus (48 BCE)**
- Pompey fled to Egypt; murdered by advisors
- Caesar campaigned in Egypt, Africa, Spain
- Complete victory by 45 BCE

**Dictatorship**:
- Appointed perpetual dictator
- Reforms: calendar, colonies for veterans, citizenship extensions
- Clemency toward former enemies
- Showed little respect for republican forms

**The Ides of March (44 BCE)**:
- Conspiracy of approximately 60 senators led by Brutus and Cassius
- Assassinated at meeting of Senate (March 15, 44 BCE)
- Conspirators expected republic to revive automatically

## The Second Triumvirate and End of the Republic

### Mark Antony, Lepidus, and Octavian

After Caesar's death:
- **Mark Antony**: Caesar's lieutenant; claimed leadership
- **Octavian** (later Augustus): Caesar's adopted heir; only 18 years old
- **Lepidus**: Caesar's master of horse

**The Second Triumvirate (43 BCE)**:
- Legally established (unlike the first) with dictatorial powers
- Proscriptions killed thousands, including Cicero
- **Battle of Philippi (42 BCE)**: Defeated Brutus and Cassius

### Division and Conflict

The triumvirs divided the Roman world:
- Antony: East (and relationship with Cleopatra)
- Octavian: West
- Lepidus: Africa (later sidelined)

Growing rivalry led to final conflict:
- **Battle of Actium (31 BCE)**: Octavian defeated Antony and Cleopatra
- Antony and Cleopatra committed suicide (30 BCE)
- Egypt annexed; Octavian controlled entire Roman world

### The Augustan Settlement (27 BCE)

Octavian "restored the Republic" while maintaining power:
- Received title **Augustus** ("Revered One")
- Held *tribunicia potestas* and *imperium proconsulare maius*
- Controlled army and provinces
- Maintained republican appearances while exercising monarchical power

The Republic was effectively over; the Principate had begun.

## Causes of the Republic's Fall

Historians debate the relative importance of:
- Structural factors (army reforms, wealth inequality)
- Individual agency (Caesar's ambition, Pompey's miscalculations)
- Ideological failure (aristocratic competition, violence)
- Success itself (empire too large for republican government)
