# Hellenistic Philosophy: Stoics, Epicureans, and Skeptics

## Overview

The Hellenistic period (323-31 BCE) witnessed a transformation in philosophy. As the intimate world of the polis gave way to vast kingdoms, new philosophical schools arose offering guidance for living well in an uncertain world. Stoicism, Epicureanism, and Skepticism became the dominant philosophies, emphasizing personal tranquility rather than political participation.

## The New Philosophical Context

### From Polis to Cosmopolis

The collapse of the city-state as the center of Greek life prompted new questions:
- How should one live in a vast, cosmopolitan world?
- What can bring happiness when political participation is limited?
- How does one find security in uncertain times?

The new philosophies offered:
- **Practical ethics**: Focus on how to live
- **Individual salvation**: Personal tranquility as the goal
- **Cosmopolitanism**: Citizenship of the world
- **Therapy of the passions**: Philosophy as medicine for the soul

## The Major Schools

### Athens: Philosophical Capital

Athens remained the philosophical center despite political decline:
- **Academy**: Continued Plato's tradition
- **Lyceum**: Aristotle's school
- **Stoa**: Stoic school, named for the *Stoa Poikile* (Painted Colonnade)
- **Garden**: Epicurus's school

## Stoicism

### Founders

- **Zeno of Citium** (c. 335-263 BCE): Cyprian who founded the school c. 300 BCE
- **Cleanthes** (c. 330-230 BCE): Second head; author of *Hymn to Zeus*
- **Chrysippus** (c. 280-207 BCE): Systematized Stoic doctrine

### Core Doctrines

**Physics (Nature)**:
- The cosmos is a living, rational being
- **Logos**: Divine reason pervading all things
- **Pneuma**: Material "breath" that structures reality
- Everything happens according to fate (*heimarmene*)
- Cyclical conflagration and renewal of the cosmos

**Logic**:
- Developed propositional logic
- Theory of knowledge: reliable impressions (*katalepsis*)
- Language and meaning

**Ethics**:
- **Virtue is the only good**; vice the only evil
- External things (health, wealth) are "preferred indifferents"
- Living according to nature = living according to reason
- **Apatheia**: Freedom from destructive passions
- The Sage (*sophos*): Ideal of perfect wisdom

**The Passions**:
Stoics classified harmful passions:
- **Desire** (*epithumia*): Irrational wanting
- **Fear** (*phobos*): Irrational avoidance
- **Pleasure** (*hedone*): Irrational elation
- **Distress** (*lupe*): Irrational contraction

These arise from false judgments about what is good or evil.

**Cosmopolitanism**:
> "I am a citizen of the world" (*kosmopolites*)

All humans share in the divine reason; boundaries of city and nation are conventional.

### Later Stoics

Roman Stoicism flourished:
- **Seneca** (c. 4 BCE-65 CE): Philosopher-statesman
- **Epictetus** (c. 50-135 CE): Former slave; author of *Enchiridion*
- **Marcus Aurelius** (121-180 CE): Emperor; *Meditations*

## Epicureanism

### Epicurus (341-270 BCE)

Born on Samos, Epicurus founded his school in Athens (307 BCE). He taught in the "Garden" (*kepos*), welcoming women and slaves—unusual for ancient schools.

### Physics

Building on Democritean atomism:
- Reality consists of atoms and void
- Atoms moving, colliding, forming temporary combinations
- The soul is material, dissolving at death
- Gods exist but do not intervene in human affairs

### Ethics

**Pleasure (*hedone*) is the highest good**, but Epicurus meant specifically:
- **Ataraxia**: Tranquility, freedom from anxiety
- **Aponia**: Absence of bodily pain

The goal is *not* sensory indulgence:
> "When we say pleasure is the goal, we do not mean the pleasures of the profligate... but freedom from bodily pain and mental disturbance."

**Classification of Desires**:
- **Natural and necessary**: Food, shelter
- **Natural but unnecessary**: Luxurious food
- **Neither natural nor necessary**: Fame, power

**The Tetrapharmakos** (Four-Part Remedy):
1. God is not to be feared
2. Death is not to be worried about
3. What is good is easy to attain
4. What is terrible is easy to endure

### Social Philosophy

- **The Garden**: Community of friends
- Withdrawal from political life
- "Live unnoticed" (*lathe biosas*)
- Friendship as the greatest good after wisdom

## Skepticism

### Pyrrho of Elis (c. 365-275 BCE)

The founder of ancient Skepticism traveled with Alexander to India. He left no writings; his thought was preserved by his student Timon.

### Core Ideas

**Epochē** (suspension of judgment):
- For every argument, an equal counter-argument can be found
- Since we cannot determine truth, we should suspend judgment
- Suspending judgment brings tranquility (*ataraxia*)

**Modes of Skepticism**:
The later Skeptic Aenesidemus listed ten modes showing why we cannot trust our judgments:
1. Different animals perceive differently
2. Individual humans differ
3. The senses give conflicting information
4. Circumstances alter perception
5. Positions and distances affect appearance
...and so on.

### Academic Skepticism

The Academy under **Arcesilaus** (c. 315-240 BCE) and **Carneades** (c. 214-129 BCE) adopted skeptical positions:
- Attacked Stoic epistemology
- Nothing can be known with certainty
- Probability (*pithanon*) as practical guide

### Later Skepticism

**Sextus Empiricus** (c. 160-210 CE) preserved skeptical arguments:
- *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*
- *Against the Professors*

Skepticism influenced modern philosophy through Montaigne and Hume.

## Other Schools

### The Cynics

Heirs of Diogenes of Sinope:
- Rejection of social conventions
- Self-sufficiency (*autarkeia*)
- "Natural" life without shame
- Influenced Stoic ethics

### The Cyrenaics

Founded by Aristippus of Cyrene:
- Bodily pleasure as the good
- Present pleasure over future
- Eventually absorbed into Epicureanism

## Legacy

Hellenistic philosophy profoundly influenced:
- **Roman Culture**: Stoicism became Rome's dominant philosophy
- **Christianity**: Stoic and Platonic concepts shaped theology
- **Modern Philosophy**: Skeptical arguments, ethical theories
- **Cognitive Therapy**: Stoic techniques in modern psychology
