# Alexander the Great: Conqueror of the World

## Overview

Alexander III of Macedon (356-323 BCE), known as "the Great," created the largest empire the ancient world had seen, stretching from Greece to India. In just thirteen years of campaigning, he conquered the Persian Empire, reached the boundaries of the known world, and spread Greek culture across vast territories. His legacy shaped the Hellenistic world and continues to fascinate historians, leaders, and dreamers.

## Macedon and Philip II

### The Kingdom of Macedon

Located north of Greece, Macedon was considered semi-barbarous by Greek city-states. The kingdom was:
- Ruled by hereditary kings
- Organized around warrior aristocracy
- Greek-speaking but culturally distinct
- Rich in timber, metals, and agricultural land

### Philip II (r. 359-336 BCE)

Alexander's father transformed Macedon into a military powerhouse:
- Created the **Macedonian phalanx**: Infantry armed with 18-foot sarissa pikes
- Developed combined-arms tactics with heavy cavalry
- Conquered Greek city-states; unified Greece under Macedonian hegemony
- League of Corinth (337 BCE): Alliance for war against Persia

Philip's assassination (336 BCE) left his plans to Alexander.

## Alexander's Early Years

### Education and Character

- Born 356 BCE in Pella to Philip and Olympias of Epirus
- Tutored by Aristotle (343-340 BCE) at Mieza
- Showed early promise: tamed the "untameable" horse Bucephalas as a youth
- Led cavalry at Battle of Chaeronea (338 BCE), age 18

### Securing the Throne

After Philip's assassination:
- Eliminated rivals and potential conspirators
- Crushed rebellions in Greece; destroyed Thebes (335 BCE)
- Demonstrated ruthlessness that would mark his career

## The Persian Campaign (334-330 BCE)

### Crossing into Asia (334 BCE)

With approximately 40,000 infantry and 5,000 cavalry, Alexander crossed the Hellespont, visiting Troy and honoring Achilles—his hero.

### Battle of the Granicus (334 BCE)

First major engagement against Persian satraps:
- Alexander led cavalry charge across river
- Nearly killed in personal combat
- Complete Macedonian victory
- Most Greek cities of Asia Minor liberated

### Siege of Tyre (332 BCE)

The island fortress seemed impregnable:
- Alexander constructed a causeway from the mainland
- Seven-month siege succeeded
- 8,000 Tyrians killed; 30,000 enslaved
- Demonstrated his determination to leave no enemy in his rear

### Egypt (332-331 BCE)

Egypt welcomed Alexander as liberator from Persian rule:
- Crowned Pharaoh at Memphis
- Founded Alexandria, which would become the greatest Hellenistic city
- Journey to Siwa oasis: the oracle of Amun hailed him as son of Zeus

### Battle of Gaugamela (331 BCE)

The decisive confrontation with Darius III near Arbela:
- Darius had vastly larger forces (estimates vary wildly)
- Alexander led a decisive cavalry charge against the Persian center
- Darius fled; the Persian Empire fell
- Alexander entered Babylon, Susa, and Persepolis

### The Burning of Persepolis

Alexander burned the great Persian palace:
- Symbolic act of revenge for Xerxes's destruction of Athens (480 BCE)
- Or drunken accident? Sources disagree
- Marked the end of the Achaemenid dynasty

## Eastern Campaigns (330-323 BCE)

### Pursuit and Death of Darius (330 BCE)

Darius was murdered by his own satrap Bessus. Alexander:
- Captured and executed Bessus
- Adopted Persian royal dress and customs
- Provoked opposition from Macedonian companions

### Central Asian Campaigns (330-327 BCE)

- Conquered Bactria and Sogdiana (modern Afghanistan/Central Asia)
- Married Roxane, daughter of Sogdian chief Oxyartes
- Years of guerrilla warfare and sieges
- Execution of Philotas and murder of Cleitus the Black showed growing tensions

### The Indian Campaign (327-325 BCE)

**Battle of the Hydaspes (326 BCE)**:
- Defeated King Porus and his war elephants
- Named a city Bucephala after his beloved horse, who died there

**The Mutiny at the Hyphasis**:
- Soldiers refused to march further east
- Alexander, furious, accepted the decision
- The army turned back

### The Return (325-324 BCE)

- Terrible march through the Gedrosian Desert
- Massive casualties from heat, thirst, and starvation
- Mass marriages at Susa: Alexander and officers married Persian noblewomen

## Death and Legacy

### Babylon (323 BCE)

Alexander died in Babylon on June 10/11, 323 BCE, aged 32:
- Cause debated: typhoid fever, alcohol, poisoning?
- Famous (likely apocryphal) last words when asked to whom he left his empire: "To the strongest" (*tōi kratistōi*)

### The Empire's Fate

Without an adult heir, the empire fragmented:
- Wars of the Diadochi (successors) lasted decades
- Eventually divided among Ptolemaic Egypt, Seleucid Asia, Antigonid Macedon

### Historical Assessment

Alexander remains controversial:
- **Admirers**: Visionary unifier, spreader of Hellenic culture
- **Critics**: Brutal conqueror, megalomaniac with increasing despotism

His conquests undeniably created the conditions for the Hellenistic age.

- **Diodorus Siculus**: Universal history
