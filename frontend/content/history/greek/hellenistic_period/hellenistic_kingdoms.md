# The Hellenistic Kingdoms: A Divided Inheritance

## Overview

Following Alexander the Great's death in 323 BCE, his empire fragmented into competing kingdoms ruled by his generals and their descendants. The Hellenistic period (323-31 BCE) saw the spread of Greek culture across a vast territory, the rise of great cosmopolitan cities, and the eventual absorption of the Greek world by Rome.

## The Wars of the Successors (Diadochi)

### The Immediate Crisis

Alexander left no clear heir:
- His half-brother Philip III Arrhidaeus was mentally disabled
- His son Alexander IV was born after his death
- His generals competed for power

### Key Successors

- **Perdiccas**: Initial regent; killed 321 BCE
- **Antipater**: Regent of Macedon; died 319 BCE
- **Ptolemy**: Seized Egypt; founded Ptolemaic dynasty
- **Seleucus**: Eventually controlled Babylon and the East
- **Antigonus One-Eye**: Attempted to reunify the empire
- **Lysimachus**: Controlled Thrace
- **Cassander**: Secured Macedon; killed Alexander's son and mother

### The Battle of Ipsus (301 BCE)

A coalition defeated Antigonus, definitively ending hopes of reunification. The empire was divided among the victors.

## The Three Major Kingdoms

### Ptolemaic Egypt (323-30 BCE)

**Capital**: Alexandria

**Territory**: Egypt, Cyrenaica, Cyprus, parts of the Levant at various times

**Characteristics**:
- Most stable Hellenistic kingdom
- Ptolemies as pharaohs, worshipped as gods
- Bureaucratic administration building on Egyptian traditions
- Royal monopolies on key resources (oil, papyrus)

**The Ptolemies**:
- **Ptolemy I Soter** (323-282 BCE): Established the dynasty; founded the Library and Museum
- **Ptolemy II Philadelphus** (282-246 BCE): Cultural zenith; completed the Lighthouse
- **Ptolemy III Euergetes** (246-222 BCE): Conquered parts of Seleucid Empire
- Later Ptolemies marked by decline, civil wars, and sibling marriages

**Alexandria**:
The greatest Hellenistic city:
- Population perhaps 500,000
- The Pharos (Lighthouse): One of Seven Wonders
- The Library: Perhaps 400,000+ scrolls
- The Museum (*Mouseion*): Research institution

### The Seleucid Empire (312-63 BCE)

**Capitals**: Seleucia on the Tigris, later Antioch

**Territory**: At its height, from Anatolia to Central Asia

**Characteristics**:
- Largest but most difficult to hold
- Greek colonies throughout the East
- Multiple peoples, languages, religions
- Constant challenges from breakaway regions

**Key Rulers**:
- **Seleucus I Nicator** (312-281 BCE): Founded the dynasty
- **Antiochus I Soter** (281-261 BCE): Defended against Galatian invaders
- **Antiochus III "the Great"** (222-187 BCE): Briefly restored the empire; defeated by Rome
- **Antiochus IV Epiphanes** (175-164 BCE): Provoked the Maccabean Revolt

**Decline**:
- Parthians conquered Iran (2nd century BCE)
- Jews achieved independence under Hasmoneans
- Pompey annexed the remnant (63 BCE)

### Antigonid Macedon (276-168 BCE)

**Capital**: Pella

**Territory**: Macedon and (contested) Greece

**Characteristics**:
- Traditional Macedonian kingship
- Constant struggles against Greek leagues
- Buffer against northern barbarians

**Key Rulers**:
- **Antigonus II Gonatas** (276-239 BCE): Stabilized the kingdom
- **Philip V** (221-179 BCE): Fought Rome; lost
- **Perseus** (179-168 BCE): Final king; defeated at Pydna

**Roman Conquest**:
- Battle of Pydna (168 BCE): Rome conquered Macedon
- Greece became Roman province of Achaea (146 BCE)

## Other Hellenistic States

### The Attalids of Pergamon (282-133 BCE)

- Rose as Seleucid power waned
- Allied with Rome against Macedon
- Famous library rivaling Alexandria
- Great Altar of Zeus (now in Berlin)
- Attalus III bequeathed kingdom to Rome (133 BCE)

### Bactria and Indo-Greek Kingdoms

- Greek kingdoms in Afghanistan and northwestern India
- Lasted until 1st century CE
- Remarkable coinage with Greek and Indian scripts
- **Menander I** (c. 165-130 BCE): Subject of Buddhist text *Milinda Panha*

### The Kingdom of Pontus

- Northern Anatolia
- **Mithridates VI** (120-63 BCE): Major opponent of Rome

## Hellenistic Society

### Greek and Native

The Hellenistic world was multicultural:
- Greek elites in cities
- Native populations in countryside
- Intermarriage varied by region
- Greek language (*koine*) as lingua franca

### The Gymnasium

Central institution of Greek identity:
- Athletic training
- Literary and philosophical education
- Social hub for Greek citizens
- Spread throughout Hellenistic world

### Economy

- Vast increase in trade networks
- Standardized coinage
- New trade routes to India and Arabia
- Economic integration across former Persian territory

## The Coming of Rome

### Early Contacts

Rome became involved in the Greek East through:
- Illyrian Wars (229-219 BCE)
- Alliance with Aetolian League
- Macedonian Wars (215-168 BCE)

### Roman Conquest

- 168 BCE: Macedon defeated
- 146 BCE: Greece and Carthage destroyed
- 133 BCE: Pergamon bequeathed to Rome
- 63 BCE: Pompey organizes the East
- 30 BCE: Egypt annexed; end of Hellenistic period
