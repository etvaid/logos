# Classical Greek Philosophy: Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle

## Overview

The fifth and fourth centuries BCE witnessed an intellectual revolution in Athens that shaped Western philosophy forever. Three towering figures—Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle—asked fundamental questions about reality, knowledge, ethics, and politics that philosophers still grapple with today. Their work transformed philosophy from cosmological speculation into systematic inquiry into the human condition.

## Socrates (469-399 BCE)

### Life and Method

Socrates wrote nothing. Everything we know comes from others, primarily Plato, Xenophon, and Aristophanes. Son of a stonemason and a midwife, Socrates compared his own method to midwifery—helping others give birth to ideas.

**The Socratic Method** (*elenchus*):
1. Ask the interlocutor to define a concept (justice, courage, piety)
2. Through questioning, reveal contradictions in their definition
3. Reach *aporia* (puzzlement)—recognizing one doesn't know what one thought one knew
4. This is progress: "I know that I know nothing"

### The Examined Life

Socrates famously declared:
> "The unexamined life is not worth living for a human being." (*Apology* 38a)

He spent his days in the *agora* questioning Athenians about their beliefs—annoying the powerful, fascinating the young.

### Trial and Death (399 BCE)

Charged with impiety (*asebeia*) and corrupting the youth, Socrates was tried, convicted, and sentenced to death by drinking hemlock. His calm acceptance, as portrayed in Plato's *Phaedo*, made him philosophy's first martyr.

## Plato (428/7-348/7 BCE)

### Life and Works

An aristocrat related to prominent Athenian families, Plato became Socrates's devoted follower. After Socrates's death, he traveled before returning to found the Academy (c. 387 BCE), the ancient world's first institution of higher learning, which lasted over 900 years.

Plato wrote philosophical dialogues featuring Socrates as the main character. Thirty-five dialogues survive, traditionally grouped:
- **Early** (Socratic): *Apology*, *Crito*, *Euthyphro*, *Ion*
- **Middle** (Platonic metaphysics): *Republic*, *Phaedo*, *Symposium*, *Phaedrus*
- **Late** (critical revision): *Parmenides*, *Theaetetus*, *Sophist*, *Laws*

### The Theory of Forms

Plato's central metaphysical doctrine: the world we perceive is a shadow of a higher reality of perfect, eternal, unchanging Forms (*eide* or *ideai*).

**The Cave Allegory** (*Republic* Book 7):
Prisoners chained in a cave see only shadows on the wall. Released and dragged into sunlight, one prisoner gradually perceives the real world. Returning to the cave, he cannot convince others.

This allegory represents:
- The shadows: our sense perception
- The ascent: philosophical education
- The sun: the Form of the Good
- The return: the philosopher's duty to educate others

### The Tripartite Soul

In the *Republic*, Plato divides the soul (*psyche*) into three parts:
1. **Reason** (*logistikon*): Seeks truth; should rule
2. **Spirit** (*thumoeides*): Source of anger and ambition; should support reason
3. **Appetite** (*epithumetikon*): Bodily desires; should obey reason

Justice in the individual mirrors justice in the city—each part doing its proper work.

### Political Philosophy

The *Republic* envisions an ideal city:
- **Philosopher-kings**: Only those who know the Good should rule
- **Guardian class**: Warriors who protect the city
- **Producers**: Craftsmen and farmers
- Communism of property and family for rulers
- Careful regulation of poetry and music

### Ethics

Virtue is knowledge. No one does wrong willingly—evil results from ignorance. The virtues:
- **Wisdom**: Excellence of reason
- **Courage**: Excellence of spirit
- **Moderation**: Harmony among soul-parts
- **Justice**: Each part doing its own work

## Aristotle (384-322 BCE)

### Life

Born in Stagira (Macedonia), son of the physician to the Macedonian king, Aristotle studied at Plato's Academy for twenty years. After Plato's death, he left Athens, eventually becoming tutor to the young Alexander the Great.

Returning to Athens (335 BCE), he founded his own school, the Lyceum (also called the Peripatetic school, from the covered walkway, *peripatos*, where he taught).

### Works

Aristotle's surviving works are lecture notes, not polished publications:
- **Logic** (*Organon*): *Categories*, *Prior Analytics*, *Posterior Analytics*
- **Natural Philosophy**: *Physics*, *On the Heavens*, *On the Soul*
- **Metaphysics**: First causes and being qua being
- **Ethics**: *Nicomachean Ethics*, *Eudemian Ethics*
- **Politics**: The best political organization
- **Rhetoric and Poetics**: Persuasion and literary theory
- **Biology**: Extensive empirical studies

### Metaphysics

Aristotle rejected Plato's transcendent Forms. For Aristotle, form exists in individual things:
- **Substance** (*ousia*): The primary category; individual things
- **Form** (*morphe*) and **Matter** (*hyle*): Inseparable aspects of substances
- **Actuality** (*energeia*) and **Potentiality** (*dynamis*): Acorn is potentially an oak
- **Four Causes**: Material, formal, efficient, final

### Ethics

**Eudaimonia** (flourishing/happiness) is the highest good, achieved through virtue:
- **Virtue** (*arete*): Excellence of character, a mean between extremes
- **Courage**: Mean between cowardice and recklessness
- **Generosity**: Mean between stinginess and prodigality
- **The Practical Syllogism**: Reason guiding action

> "Virtue is a mean state... lying between two vices, one of excess and one of deficiency." (*Nicomachean Ethics* 2.6)

### Political Philosophy

Humans are "political animals" (*zoon politikon*) by nature. Aristotle analyzed 158 constitutions, classifying governments:

| Rule by... | Good Form | Corrupt Form |
|------------|-----------|--------------|
| One | Monarchy | Tyranny |
| Few | Aristocracy | Oligarchy |
| Many | Polity | Democracy |

The *Politics* advocates a mixed constitution balancing elements of different forms.

### Logic

Aristotle invented formal logic:
- **Syllogism**: "All men are mortal; Socrates is a man; therefore Socrates is mortal"
- **Categories**: Classification of predicates
- **Laws of Thought**: Identity, non-contradiction, excluded middle

### Natural Philosophy

Aristotle's biology was remarkably sophisticated:
- Classified over 500 species
- Recognized dolphins as mammals
- Studied embryology through chicken eggs
- His physics (natural place, projectile motion) was less successful

## Legacy

The three philosophers created the framework for Western thought:
- **Socrates**: The questioning spirit of philosophy
- **Plato**: Transcendent reality, idealism, higher education
- **Aristotle**: Empiricism, logic, systematic inquiry

Medieval philosophy divided into Platonists and Aristotelians. Modern philosophy often defines itself against these traditions—but always in relation to them.
